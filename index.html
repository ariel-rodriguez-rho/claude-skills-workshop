Teach Claude your job, once

A hands-on beginner workshop on Claude Skills — 45 minutes, no coding, leave with a skill you built yourself.

The problem: every time you ask Claude for help, you re-explain the same things — your format, your tone, your checklist, your team's rules. Ten minutes of setup before every answer.

The fix: a Skill is that explanation, saved. It's a small instruction file Claude automatically pulls up whenever it's relevant — in any chat, forever. Explain your weekly report format once, and every future "write my weekly report" just… comes out right.

Before the workshop5-minute setup

  • Sign in at claude.ai (or the Claude desktop app) with your Claude account
  • Open Settings → Capabilities and make sure “Code execution and file creation” is turned on — Skills need it
  • Find the Skills section in Settings — that's where you'll browse, enable, and upload them

Exercise 1 · 5 minutesWatch a skill work

Claude ships with ready-made skills for creating real files — spreadsheets, slide decks, Word docs. Make sure they're enabled in Settings, then paste this into a new chat:

Create a spreadsheet tracking 5 example vendor contracts:
vendor name, renewal date, annual cost, owner, and status.
Add a summary row and make it look clean.

You'll get a real, downloadable .xlsx file — properly formatted, formulas and all. Notice what you didn't do: explain how spreadsheets should be structured. The skill knew. That's the whole idea, and now you'll build one for your own work.

Exercise 2 · 15 minutesBuild your own — by asking

You don't write skills; you describe them, and Claude writes them. Paste this and answer its questions:

Help me create a custom skill. It's called "weekly-status".

When I give you my raw notes from the week, turn them into my
team's status update format:
- WINS (done this week)
- IN FLIGHT (with expected dates)
- BLOCKED (and who can unblock it)
- NEXT WEEK

Tone: brief and direct, no corporate filler. Interview me about
anything else you need, then package the skill as a zip file I
can download and upload to my Claude settings.

Swap in whatever chore you re-explain most — an email-triage briefing, meeting-notes formatting, a vendor-comparison checklist, your SOP for handling a request type. The pattern is always: name it, describe when it applies, show the output you want, ask for a zip.

What's actually inside the zip?

One folder with a SKILL.md file — plain text with a tiny header. That's a complete skill:

---
name: weekly-status
description: Formats raw weekly notes into the team's
  status update. Use whenever the user shares weekly
  notes or asks for a status update.
---

Turn the user's notes into this exact format:
## WINS · ## IN FLIGHT · ## BLOCKED · ## NEXT WEEK
Brief and direct. Every BLOCKED item names who can
unblock it. Ask before inventing dates.

The description is the trigger — Claude reads it to decide when the skill applies. Larger skills can include reference docs and templates in the same folder, but many great ones are just this.

Exercise 3 · 10 minutesInstall it and test it

Download the zip Claude made for you.

Go to Settings → Skills → Upload skill and upload the zip.

Open a fresh chat, paste some real notes from your week, and say “write my status update.”

No mention of the skill needed — Claude sees the request matches the skill's description and uses it on its own. If the output isn't quite right, go back to your skill-building chat, say what to change, and re-upload. That loop is skill development.

SharingOne person builds it, everyone has it

The zip is the skill — send it to a teammate and they upload it to their own settings. On Team and Enterprise plans, admins can also publish skills to the whole workspace so everyone gets them automatically.

This is the multiplier: the person who best knows how a task should be done encodes it once, and the whole team's Claude gets better at it.

⚠️ Only install skills from people you trust. Skills can include runnable code, so treat a skill zip like you'd treat a macro-enabled file: team-made and Anthropic-made are fine; random internet downloads deserve a skeptical read first.

Idea boardSkills worth building this week

  • email-triage — your rules for what's urgent, who's a VIP, and what a morning briefing should look like
  • meeting-notes — raw transcript in, your team's decisions/actions/owners format out
  • vendor-compare — the checklist you always score vendors against, applied to any quotes you paste in
  • request-intake — your SOP for triaging inbound asks: what to ask for, where it goes, standard replies
  • brand-voice — how your team writes: tone, banned phrases, sign-offs

Good to knowCommon questions

Do I need to know how to code?
No. You describe the skill in plain English and Claude writes the file. Coding only enters if you want the skill to run scripts.
How is this different from a Project?
A Project is a place (one workspace, its own docs and chats). A Skill is a portable how-to that follows you into every chat and activates itself when relevant. They combine well.
How do I know when Claude used a skill?
Claude shows when a skill is active in its response. You can also just ask: “which skills did you use?”
Can I turn one off?
Yes — every skill has a toggle in Settings. Off means Claude never sees it.
My skill didn't trigger.
Its description probably doesn't match how you phrased the request. Ask Claude to broaden the description and re-upload — or invoke it by name: “use my weekly-status skill.”
