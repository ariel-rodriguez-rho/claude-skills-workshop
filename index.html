Let Claude read your email so you don't have to

A 20-minute setup, then it runs every morning by itself — even while your laptop sleeps.

Who this is for: anyone who spends the first hour of their day digging through email. No technical skills needed — if you can write a Slack message, you can do this.

What you'll have at the end: every weekday at 7 AM, Claude reads your new email and delivers a short briefing — what needs you today, what's waiting on others, and what you can safely ignore. Reading it takes about a minute.

Before you start2-minute checklist

  • Claude Desktop app installed (Mac or Windows) — claude.ai/download — and sign in with your Claude account
  • Your work Gmail login handy

⚠️ Use your work account only. Claude processes email content on Anthropic's servers. Don't connect a personal inbox, and check your company's policy if you're unsure.

Part 1 · 5 minutes, one time onlyConnect your Gmail

Open the Claude Desktop app and switch to Cowork mode.

Open Connectors (in settings) and find Gmail.

Click to authorize, sign in with your work Google account, and grant access.

That's it. Claude can now read your inbox — but only when you ask it to, and it never sends anything without your approval.

Part 2 · The “wow” momentYour first triage

Paste this into Cowork and watch it work:

Look at my unread email from the last 24 hours and give me a briefing:

1. NEEDS ME TODAY — anything I have to reply to or act on, with a
   one-line summary each
2. WAITING ON OTHERS — things I'm waiting for, and from whom
3. FYI ONLY — one line each
4. SAFE TO IGNORE — just count them

Keep it short. Bullet points, no fluff.

It takes a couple of minutes — Cowork works step by step, like a person would. What you get back is the map of your morning. Best demoed on a real inbox with 20+ unread emails.

Part 3 · The actual magicMake it automatic

Now paste this:

Schedule that same email briefing to run every weekday at 7:00 AM.

Claude sets up the schedule itself. Confirm the details it shows you, and you're done.

Key facts about scheduled tasks:

  • They run remotely on Anthropic's servers — your computer can be asleep or the app closed.
  • Each morning's briefing is waiting for you in Cowork.
  • To see what's scheduled, just ask: “Show me my scheduled tasks.”
  • To stop one: “Cancel my morning email task.” Everything is conversation — no settings menus.

Part 4 · Optional, for laterLevel up when you're ready

Don't do these on day one. Once the morning briefing has earned your trust, add one at a time.

Teach it your inbox (run once — makes everything smarter) 
Scan my Gmail from the past 30 days. Identify my most frequent senders
and what category they fall into, the types of requests I get most
often, and how I typically reply to each. Remember this for future
email briefings.
Pre-drafted replies, waiting for your review 
Update my morning briefing task: for routine requests (scheduling,
acknowledgments, simple questions), also draft a reply I can review.
Never send anything — drafts only.
Follow-up catcher — who owes you a reply? 
Create a scheduled task for weekdays at 7:05 AM: scan my sent mail
from the last 14 days for emails that never got a reply. List who
owes me a response and what for, and suggest a short nudge for each.
VIP alerts — never bury the boss 
Update my morning briefing: emails from [boss's name], [key partner],
or anyone @importantclient.com always appear first, under a
"HIGH SIGNAL" heading.

Good to knowCommon questions

Can it send email without me?
No. It drafts; you approve and send.
What if the briefing gets something wrong?
Tell it — “that vendor email was actually urgent” — and it adjusts the task. It's a conversation, not a config file.
Does my laptop need to be on at 7 AM?
No. Scheduled tasks run in the cloud.
Is this reading ALL my email forever?
Only what the task describes (e.g., unread from the last 24 hours), only on the schedule you set, and you can cancel anytime.
I got stuck.
Ask Claude itself: “Walk me through connecting Gmail” — it will guide you.

You describe the chore once, in plain English.
It happens every morning, forever — and you get your first hour back.
